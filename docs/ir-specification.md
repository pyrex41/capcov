A command runs its actions as one transaction (`caps.run`,
`system.shen:150-160`). Each action sees the transaction's intermediate state;
`publish` appends `[emit Kind V]` to the transaction's effects
(`system.shen:67`). The commit decision is `caps.finish`
(`system.shen:144-148`): the transaction commits only if every action
succeeded and the final state satisfies schema, invariants and postcondition;
otherwise it is rejected as a whole. A request names an observed outcome, and
`caps.admissible` (`system.shen:192-193`, through the bridge of section 7 to
`caps.adm`, `system.shen:167-174`) maps it to the alternatives an observation
must be one of:

| Request outcome | Model commits `(S', E)` | Model rejects |
|---|---|---|
| `committed` | `{(S', E)}` | `{}` |
| `rejected` | `{}` | `{(S, [])}` |
| `aborted` | `{(S, [])}` | `{(S, [])}` |
| `unknown` | `{(S, []), (S', E)}` | `{(S, [])}` |

`unknown` has at most two alternatives, resolved inside its own transition.
That rests on two assumptions v1 leaves implicit: `publish` commits with the
transaction, and an unknown outcome resolves in place (section 7 names them).

### 4.1 Outcomes

An invocation `x` of operation `O` with arguments `a` completes with one
outcome. `S_x` is the state at `x`'s linearization point; when earlier `info`
invocations are still open (4.4) it ranges over their interleavings, and
everything below is evaluated in each. `Run(O, a, S_x)` is the set of complete
executions; each ends `committed(S', J)` or `rejected(L)`. An execution is a
sequence of **commit points**: a transaction commit, a delivery claim, an
attempt send (4.3). `Adm(x)` is the set of `(state, journal)` alternatives an
observation of `x` must be one of.

| Outcome | `Adm(x)` | A checker may conclude | A checker may not conclude |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[ok]` | every `committed(S', J)` in `Run` | `x`'s transactions committed; each delivery took one of its response arms | that an attempt with an `info` response landed, or did not |
| `[fail [rejected L]]` | `{(S_x, [])}` if the model rejects with `L` in `S_x`, else `{}` | under `definite-fail-v1`: `x` changed nothing and emitted nothing | anything about other invocations; anything without the assumption |
| `[fail [aborted]]` | `{(S_x, [])}` whatever `Run` does | under `definite-fail-v1`: `x` changed nothing | that the model would have rejected, or committed |
| `[info]` | `{(S_x, [])}` ∪ every prefix, at commit points, of every committed execution, each sent attempt landed or not | `x`'s effects, if any, are one of these; an observed effect of `x` is real | that `x` had no effect; that its effects precede any later read; any one alternative chosen for convenience |

**Definiteness is an assumption.** A `fail` outcome is a classifier's
judgment that nothing happened. `capcov.faults` defaults every exception to
`info` (`classify_unknown`, `faults/history.py:78`) and its fake service
classifies only `Refused` as `fail` (`faults/service.py:58`). R02 names this
`definite-fail-v1:<classifier-id>`, where the id names the adapter's
classifier. Every result that uses a `fail` outcome carries it, and its
conformance and clause verdicts are conditional on it. A history without it
reads every `fail` as `info`.

**Rejection is part of the commit decision.** A transaction commits iff all
its actions succeed and the resulting state satisfies schema and invariants,
as `caps.finish` decides today. A command's postcondition is part of that
decision too, as in v1. An operation's postcondition is checked at its
completion; failing it is the model defect `postcondition`, not a rejection.

**Late rejection is a model defect (R-LATE-REJECT).** Any rejection in a
transaction that is not the operation's first step — a failed `require`,
`duplicate-key` from `insert` or `outbox`, `update`/`remove` of a missing row,
a schema or invariant failure — is reported by evaluation as the model defect
`late-rejection`, a finding against the model, never as an outcome or an
alternative. After a commit point the invocation cannot truthfully complete
`fail`. Chosen over a static rule because no syntactic rule covers missing
rows or invariants; the cost is that the defect is found only on explored
paths.

Negative: `rejected-history-unknown-outcome.ops-v2` (T-outcome); M3 (`ok`
reported for an `info` send that did not land: `Adm(ok)` excludes it).

### 4.2 Intent and attempt identity

- **Intent.** `[intent X]` on an operation, an outbox record or a delivery.
  `X` uses only `literal`, `argument`, and `row-key` inside `for-each`
  (R-INTENT-SCOPE), so a retry with the same arguments has the same intent and
  no intent depends on state. A v1 `command` has no intent: its intent id is
  its invocation id.
- **Attempt.** Not syntax. A step's attempt id is the invocation id joined
  with the step name, `Inv/step`; a command's single step is `body`. Step
  names are unique per operation, action names likewise, in separate
  namespaces (R-NAMES). A history's `invoke` supplies `Inv`, so a re-invocation
  is a new attempt of the same intent.
- **Effect record.** `[effect I A L]`. `L` is one of v1's runtime effects,
  now typed: `create`, `put`, `delete`, `emit`. Outbox rows are created with
  `create`; a landed delivery is an `emit` in the channel's journal. `emit` is
  kept because the v1 bridge must stay byte-identical (section 7); nothing
  needs a separate "delivered" effect.
- **Duplicate.** For a channel declared `[at-most-once]` and intent `i`: two
  or more entries `[effect i _ [emit Kind _]]` in the channel's journal ⇒
  `effect_applied_twice(Kind, i)`. Entries count by journal position, not by
  value. Equal payloads under different intents are not a duplicate (M2);
  different payloads under one intent are. Under `[at-least-once]`, repeated
  deliveries are not a violation.

A checker may count observed `emit` entries per `(Kind, i)` as a lower bound
and conclude a duplicate from two of them. It may not identify intents by
payload, and may not conclude "at most one" from a journal that has a gap or
is not drained (4.5).

Negative: `rejected-effect-without-intent.ops-v2` (T-outbox),
`rejected-intent-reads-state.ops-v2` (R-INTENT-SCOPE),
`rejected-untagged-effect.ops-v2` (T-effect). At the engine's front end
(PR 1), a request that names its invocation must report every observed effect
as a record; an untagged one is refused (`test_r02_bridge`).

### 4.3 Transactions, outbox records, delivery attempts

- `[channel Kind Contract T]`, in the table list, declares channel `Kind`
  with delivery contract `[at-most-once]` or `[at-least-once]`, and its
  outbox table `T` with fields `payload` and `state` (`pending` |
  `in-flight` | `delivered`), keyed by intent id. R-CHANNEL: `outbox` and
  `deliver` name a channel's table; a table has at most one channel and is not
  also declared plain; `insert` and `remove` never name it; `update` of it
  writes only `state`, to one of the three literals.
- `[outbox T [intent X] V]`, a v1 action: inside its transaction it creates
  row `i = ⟦X⟧` of `T` with `payload ⟦V⟧`, `state pending`
  (`[create T i [["payload" v] ["state" "pending"]]]`). Write set `{T}`.
  `duplicate-key` if the row exists, as `insert`. R-EFFECT-PLACEMENT:
  `outbox` only in an operation, `publish` only in a command.
- `[transaction N As]`, a step: `As` commit or reject as a whole (4.1); one
  commit point.
- `[deliver N T [intent X] [on-ok Ok] [on-fail Fail] [on-info Info]]`, an
  **external, non-transactional** step. With `i = ⟦X⟧`, `r = T[i]`, and
  `Eligible` = `{pending}` for at-most-once, `{pending, in-flight}` for
  at-least-once:
  1. If `r` is absent or `r.state ∉ Eligible`, the step does nothing.
  2. **Claim**: commit `state := in-flight` (a commit point).
  3. **Send** attempt `Inv/N` with `r.payload` (a commit point).
  4. The environment chooses the response. `ok`: the attempt landed before
     the response; the channel's journal holds `[effect i Inv/N [emit Kind payload]]`;
     run `Ok`. `fail`: the attempt never lands, a channel property assumed
     under `definite-fail-v1:<classifier-id>` for the system's own client
     of that channel; run `Fail`. `info`: it lands at any later point,
     including after the final read, or never; run `Info`.
  5. **One landing**: an attempt lands at most once. This is named
     `single-landing-v1` now, declared by any system with a channel; its
     semantics lands in PR 3. A service that applies one request twice
     (`ApplyTwice` in `capcov.faults`) violates it.

All three arms are required (T-deliver). The arm names are not outcome tags:
`on-ok` is the system's reaction to its own call's response.

**`info` send, at least three alternatives.** `send` of
`transfer-outbox.ops-v2` from job `j` pending, no outbox row. Writing
`(job status, outbox state, emit entries for j)`:

| Alternative | State | Outbox journal suffix | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| a0 | `(pending, —, 0)` | none | nothing |
| a1 | `(queued, pending, 0)` | create | outbox-only |
| a2 | `(queued, in-flight, 0)` | create, put in-flight (attempt sent or not; if sent, may still land) | outbox-only |
| a5 | `(queued, pending, 0)` | create, put in-flight, put pending (`on-fail`) | outbox-only |
| a3 | `(queued, in-flight, 1)` | create, put in-flight; landed | outbox+delivered |
| a4 | `(queued, delivered, 1)` | create, put in-flight, put delivered (`on-ok`) | outbox+delivered |

`Adm(info) = {a0, a1, a2, a3, a4, a5}`; `Adm(ok) = {a2, a3, a4, a5}` (complete
executions, one per response; after an `info` response a2 has an open
attempt); `Adm(fail [aborted]) = {a0}`; `Adm(fail [rejected L]) = {}`, since
`send` rejects only in its precondition, which holds.

Negative: `rejected-outbox-into-plain-table.ops-v2`,
`rejected-insert-into-channel-table.ops-v2` (R-CHANNEL);
`rejected-deliver-without-info-arm.ops-v2` (T-deliver);
`rejected-publish-in-operation.ops-v2` (R-EFFECT-PLACEMENT);
`rejected-publish-outside-transaction.ops-v2` (T-step).

### 4.4 Histories

`[history Es]`. R-HISTORY: `[open]` first and `[close]` last, once each;
invocation ids unique; each `[complete Inv O]` follows its
`[invoke Inv Op Args]`, at most once; an invocation not completed by `[close]`
is `info`, as `History.close()` does. **No overlap** (decided, until R17): an
`invoke` may not occur while another invocation is invoked and not completed;
an invocation completed `info` counts as completed for this rule. Any sequence
of invocations meeting these is a legal history, whatever a retry policy
says: retry constrains exploration only (section 12), and re-invoking after
`ok` is legal (decided).

- An invocation completed `ok` or `fail` has all its commit points between its
  invoke and its complete; its attempts with an `info` response may land later.
- An invocation completed `info` stays **open until close**: each of its
  remaining commit points may occur at any later point, interleaved at commit
  points with later invocations, after any observation, and after `[close]`.
- A **world** is an interleaving of every invocation's commit points
  consistent with the above, each transaction evaluated in the interleaved
  state. Exploration bounds worlds; exhausting a bound is `unresolved`.

For commands under `info-in-place-v1` (section 7) an `info` invocation has no
later commit points: it resolves at its own position, as in v1.

Negative: `rejected-history-without-close.ops-v2`,
`rejected-history-overlapping-invocations.ops-v2` (R-HISTORY); H3.

### 4.5 Observations

`[observation B Reads]`, an event of the history.

- `B` (boolean): the reads form one cut (a barrier makes them mutually
  consistent). Without it no judgment combines two stores.
- `[rows T D Rows]`: the full content of table `T`.
- `[journal S D From To Parts]`: positions `From..To` of store `S`'s own
  journal (a table's change log, or a channel's delivery log). A channel has
  no rows; its content is its journal. Positions are the source's dense
  sequence (binlog position, change-log sequence, broker offset), never
  assigned by the adapter. Each position in `From..To` is covered exactly
  once, by `[entry Seq E]` or `[gap A B]`; positions below `From` count as a
  gap (R-JOURNAL; otherwise the observation is malformed). A journal may hold
  entries from outside the history: other producers on a shared channel.
- `D` (boolean, per store): `S` is drained, no write or landing pending. `To`
  is the end of `S`'s journal only when `D`; otherwise entries may follow.
- Order: positions order entries within one store. Across stores there is
  no order, except that under a barrier every read entry precedes the cut.

Rules, each a limit on what a checker may conclude:

- O1. **Gap contents are unobserved reality**, not constrained by the model:
  the system under test may have done anything there, and the position may
  hold another producer's entry. The set of realities consistent with a
  gapped journal is unbounded, so any judgment its contents could change is
  `unresolved`. Only judgments established by observed facts alone stand: two
  observed `emit` entries for one at-most-once intent; an observed entry no
  world produces.
- O2. Without a barrier, nothing combining two stores is concluded.
- O3. In a store not drained, an unobserved write or landing is not absent; it
  may occur after the read.
- O4. Entries are distinct by position. Two equal records at two positions
  are two effects; an adapter that merges them leaves a position uncovered
  (R-JOURNAL). This defends against M4 **only if the range is the source's**:
  a set-normalizing adapter that also rewrites the range to `1..1` is
  consistent and passes R-JOURNAL. The harness must take `From`/`To` from the
  store, not from the adapter's report.

Negative: `rejected-journal-merged-entries.ops-v2` (R-JOURNAL); H3, H4.

### 4.6 Verdicts

Every verdict names its executor class and carries the assumption list: the
model's declared assumptions plus `definite-fail-v1:<classifier-id>` when a
`fail` outcome or an `on-fail` arm was used.

- **Conformance** of a history to a model. `mismatch`: no world is consistent
  with the observed facts (sound under gaps, since observed facts only add).
  `matches`: some world is consistent and every read is complete (barrier,
  every store drained, no gap). `unresolved` otherwise.
- **Clause**, over the worlds consistent with the observations, unknowns
  completed every allowed way (gaps unboundedly, O1). `violated`: observed
  facts alone falsify it. `satisfied`: the world set is non-empty and the
  clause holds in every world. `unresolved` otherwise; in particular, a
  clause over zero consistent worlds is `unresolved` unless observed facts
  alone falsify it.
- **Mismatch dominates.** When conformance is `mismatch`, the only clause
  verdicts reported are those observed facts alone establish as `violated`;
  every other clause is `unresolved`, because no world of the model explains
  the history.
- Clause names are faults invariants where one exists:
  `effect_applied_twice(Kind, i)`; `unsettled_effect(T, i)` when outbox row
  `i` is not `delivered`, `violated` only when `T` and the channel are read
  under a barrier and drained.
- **Model exploration** (no observation): a *finding* when some world within
  the bound violates the clause; otherwise "no finding within bound", never a
  proof.

### 4.7 Rules

`T-*` are typing judgments of the overlay. `R-*` are side conditions of
`caps.scoped?` or of history and observation checking, specified here and
implemented by their PR.

| Rule | Where | Rejects |
|---|---|---|
| `T-outcome` | `caps-outcome` | an outcome other than `ok`, `fail R`, `info` |
| `T-effect` | `caps-effect` | a bare logical effect where an effect record `[effect I A L]` is required (no intent id, no attempt id) |
| `T-outbox` | `caps-action-outbox` | an outbox record whose key is not `[intent X]` |
| `T-step` | `caps-step` | a step that is neither a transaction nor a delivery |
| `T-deliver` | `caps-step` | a delivery without all three arms `on-ok`, `on-fail`, `on-info` |
| `R-INTENT-SCOPE` | `caps.scoped?` | an intent expression using anything but `literal`, `argument`, `row-key` |
| `R-NAMES` | `caps.scoped?` | duplicate step names, or duplicate action names, in one operation |
| `R-CHANNEL` | `caps.scoped?` | `outbox`/`deliver` not naming a channel's table; two channels on one table; a channel table also declared plain; `insert`/`remove`/non-`state` `update` of a channel table |
| `R-EFFECT-PLACEMENT` | `caps.scoped?` | `publish` in an operation; `outbox` in a command |

## 6. Composition derivation and rejected examples

Judgment `{P} A {Q} ▷ W; E`: from `P`, `A` reaches `Q`, writing tables `W`,
appending effects `E` in order. Rules used (draft; the rule ledger and checker
are R04):

- P-update: `{Q[T.k.F ↦ v] ∧ exists T k} [update T K F V] {Q} ▷ {T}; [put T k F v]`
- P-insert: `{¬exists T k ∧ Q[T.k ↦ Fs]} [insert T K Fs] {Q} ▷ {T}; [create T k Fs]`
- P-outbox: `{¬exists T i ∧ Q[T.i ↦ (v, pending)]} [outbox T [intent X] V] {Q} ▷ {T}; [create T i [payload v, state pending]]`
- Seq: `{P} A {Q} ▷ W1; E1` and `{Q} B {R} ▷ W2; E2` give `{P} A;B {R} ▷ W1 ∪ W2; E1·E2`
- Frame: `{P} A {Q} ▷ W; E` and `tables(F) ∩ W = ∅` give `{P ∧ F} A {Q ∧ F} ▷ W; E`
- Conseq: strengthen the precondition, weaken the postcondition.

**Derivation: `core ; notify`** in the fresh branch of `transfer`'s `apply`,
where `core = funds; debit; credit; record` and
`notify = [outbox "transfer-outbox" [intent [argument "request"]] [argument "request"]]`.
Let `r` be the request.

1. From `ledger.ops`: `{P0} core {Q1} ▷ {wallet, transfer}; [put wallet from, put wallet to, create transfer r]`,
   with `P0` = precondition ∧ `¬exists transfer r` ∧ funds, and `Q1` =
   `exists transfer r` ∧ balances moved by `amount` ∧ `sum(wallet)` unchanged.
2. Invariant `outbox-follows-transfer` holds in the pre-state, so
   `¬exists transfer r ⇒ ¬exists transfer-outbox r`. Conseq:
   `{P0 ∧ F} core {Q1}` with `F = ¬exists transfer-outbox r`.
3. `tables(F) = {transfer-outbox}`, disjoint from `W(core) = {wallet, transfer}`.
   Frame: `{P0 ∧ F} core {Q1 ∧ F}`.
4. P-outbox with `Q = Q1 ∧ exists transfer-outbox r ∧ state pending`;
   `tables(Q1) ∩ {transfer-outbox} = ∅`, so `Q1` is framed through:
   `{Q1 ∧ F} notify {Q1 ∧ exists transfer-outbox r ∧ pending} ▷ {transfer-outbox}; [create transfer-outbox r …]`.
5. Seq on 3 and 4: `{P0 ∧ F} core; notify {Q1 ∧ exists transfer-outbox r ∧ pending}`
   `▷ {wallet, transfer, transfer-outbox}; [put, put, create, create]`.
6. Commit decision (4.1): the invariants hold in the final state —
   `outbox-follows-transfer` from `exists transfer r`, `nonnegative` from
   `funds` — so the transaction commits and every record is tagged
   `(r, Inv/apply)`.

Why it is valid: the middle assertion of Seq matches after Conseq; both Frame
side conditions are checked on computed write sets; effects compose by
concatenation, keeping order and multiplicity (a set union would be M4); one
transaction gives every record one intent and one attempt. What it rests on:
the access summary that `outbox` writes its table. Summarized like v1
`publish` (empty write set, `system.shen:260`), step 3 would still hold, but
any later frame or commutation claim involving `transfer-outbox` would be
unsound (section 12). The `deliver` step after `apply` is not part of the
composition: it is past a commit point, and the `info` prefixes of 4.1 are
the cuts there.

**Rejected examples.** Each is a fixture in `v2-draft/` whose first line names
the rejecting rule. `T-*` fixtures must fail typing in `DraftTypecheckTest`;
`R-*` fixtures must type, so only their side condition can reject them.

| Fixture | Rule | What acceptance would allow |
|---|---|---|
| `rejected-effect-without-intent.ops-v2` | `T-outbox` | an outbox record whose duplicates can be stated only by payload |
| `rejected-deliver-without-info-arm.ops-v2` | `T-deliver` | an unhandled `info` response treated as success |

## 7. Assumptions and compatibility

Assumptions are plain strings, named and versioned. A system that names its
assumptions declares them; a result carries the model's plus the history's.

| Assumption | Statement | Declared by |
|---|---|---|
| `info-in-place-v1` | an `info` outcome of a command resolves at its own position: no later commit point, no landing after a read | systems with a command |
| `atomic-publish-v1` | a command's `publish` commits with its transaction | systems with a publishing command |
| `single-landing-v1` | an attempt lands at most once (semantics in PR 3) | systems with a channel |
| `definite-fail-v1:<classifier-id>` | a `fail` outcome, and an `on-fail` response, mean nothing happened, as judged by the named classifier | the history's adapter; carried by results |

R-ASSUME rejects an unknown name and a system that names its assumptions but
lacks one its commands or channels need. Splitting the old single
"atomic publish" into the first two was decided: they are independent, and
the second is about histories, not publish.

**Bridge.** A v1 system `[system N Ts Cs Ps]` is read as
`[system N As Ts Cs Ps]` with `As` = `info-in-place-v1` if it has a command,
plus `atomic-publish-v1` if a command publishes: exactly what R-ASSUME
requires of a system that declares its assumptions. A command is an operation without
intent, with the single step `[transaction "body" As]`. Request outcomes:
`committed` ⇒ `[ok]`; `rejected` ⇒ `[fail [rejected L]]` with `L` bound to the
model's own rejection label, so any label is accepted as v1 does
(`caps.bridge-outcome`, `system.shen:184-190`); `aborted` ⇒ `[fail [aborted]]`; `unknown` ⇒ `[info]`. The
request API forces outcomes, so `definite-fail-v1` holds by construction
there. A v1 effect `L` of invocation `Inv` is `[effect Inv Inv/body L]`;
`π([effect I A L]) = L`.

**As implemented (PR 1).** `caps.admissible` is `caps.adm` (the table of 4.1
for a command) applied to `caps.bridge-outcome` of the request's outcome, so
every v1 request is answered through the bridge. A request may name its
outcome in the R02 alphabet, as the JSON array of its S-expression (`["ok"]`,
`["fail", ["rejected", L]]`, `["fail", ["aborted"]]`, `["info"]`), and may name
an `invocation` id; an invocation id must not contain `/`, the attempt-id
separator, and a rejection label is nonempty (the bridge's empty label is
internal). Only such a request's result carries `assumptions`: the model's
declared list, or for a v1 system the bridge's (`caps.assumptions`), plus
`definite-fail-v1:request` when the outcome is `fail`. The request API forces
its outcome, so the request is the classifier; the name says so instead of
leaving the assumption implicit. With an
`invocation`, the admissible effects are records `[effect Inv Inv/body L]`
(`caps.identify`), an observation must report records (an untagged effect is
refused), an observed record with another intent or attempt id does not match,
and the independent checker (`verify_effects`) checks each record's ids and
replays the projections. `explore` with an R02 outcome carries, at the top
of the campaign, the union of its results' assumptions: the model's, plus
`definite-fail-v1:request` when some call fails. The v1 adapter protocols
(`capcov-system-adapter-v1`, `capcov-scenario-adapter-v1`) carry v1 requests
only; R02 outcomes reach adapters with `capcov-scenario-adapter-v2` (PR 4).
A system `[system N As Ts Cs Ps]` is evaluated like its v1 form, with
R-ASSUME as a side condition of `caps.scoped?`: `As` names only
`info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1`, `single-landing-v1`, each once, and
includes those its commands need.

**Claim.** For every v1 request with outcome `committed`, `rejected` or
`aborted`, `π(Adm(bridge(req))) = caps.admissible(req)`: a command has one
transaction and no delivery, so `Run` is exactly the one execution `caps.run`
computes. For `unknown`, one transaction is one commit point, so the `info`
prefixes are exactly `{nothing, everything}` (`system.shen:173-174`), under
`info-in-place-v1`, and under `atomic-publish-v1` for a publishing command.

**Gate (G5), from PR 1.** In the `shen` family, every existing `*.ops`
fixture's `evaluate` and `explore` JSON is byte-identical before and after each
R02 PR. The v1 request API's output therefore gains no assumption field;
`capcov-system-evaluation-v1` implies the bridge's assumptions, and only R02
requests and history verdicts carry them. Enforced by
`tests.claim_semantics.test_v1_compatibility.V1CompatibilityGateTest` against
goldens written at the R02 base commit, whose engine digests are a constant of the test (`BASE_LIBRARIES`); regeneration is refused on any other engine. Two values are
outside the comparison: `libraries`, the digests of the engine's own Shen
sources, which an R02 PR changes by definition, and `runtime`, the digests of
the host's binaries. Every other byte, and the exit code, is compared. The
bridge's claim below is checked by `test_r02_bridge` on the same inputs: each
v1 request rewritten to its R02 outcome answers exactly as the golden, plus
`assumptions` as in the table.

| Fixture | Commands | Publishing commands | committed / aborted / rejected | `unknown` → `info` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `outbox.ops` | `enqueue`, `send`, `ack` | `send` | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1` |
| `outbox-intended.ops` | `enqueue`, `send`, `ack` | `send` | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1` |
| `ledger.ops` | `transfer` | `transfer` | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1` |
| `workspace.ops` | `create`, `rename`, `close`, `delete-account` | `create`, `close` | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1` |
| `loop-completion.ops` | `close-all` | none | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1` |
| `loop-induction.ops` | `close-all` | none | identical | `{nothing, everything}` under `info-in-place-v1` |

Consequence for the probe: a v1 fixture keeps its v1 answer, so an IR-gap
test that flips must flip on the R02 model, not on a v1 fixture.

## 11. Decisions and open questions

Decided by the owner (via the coordinator) and recorded here:

- **Assumption split**: `atomic-publish-v1` and `info-in-place-v1`, both for
  commands, the second for every v1 fixture, the first additionally for
  publishing commands. Rationale: independent claims about different things.
- **No overlapping invocations** until R17 (R-HISTORY). Rationale: the
  interleaving semantics for concurrent `ok` invocations is R17's.
- **Wire form**: the fixture S-expressions as JSON arrays verbatim, protocol
  `capcov-scenario-adapter-v2`. Rationale: one shape for fixtures, adapters
  and the Shen term; no second schema to drift.
- **Retry after `ok`** is a legal history. Rationale: histories record what
  happened; retry policy only shapes exploration.
- **`single-landing-v1`** named now, semantics in PR 3.
- **Drain is per store.** Rationale: matches the faults completeness flags
  (`applied_closed`, `changes_closed`, `finals_closed`) and real deployments,
  where one store can be quiescent while another is not.

Spec deviations in PR 1, each the minimal correct reading:

- **G5 cannot cover the engine's own digests.** `evaluate` reports
  `libraries`, the sha256 of `system.shen` and `system-types.shen`, which any
  R02 PR changes; `runtime` is the host binaries'. The gate compares every
  other byte and names the two exclusions.
- **Results carrying assumptions.** Section 4.6 says every verdict carries the
  assumption list; section 7 says the v1 request API gains no assumption
  field. Both hold: a v1 request's result is unchanged, a request using an R02
  outcome or an invocation id carries `assumptions`. A `fail` outcome adds
  `definite-fail-v1:request`: the request API forces outcomes, so the
  classifier is the request itself, and the result names it (section 4.1:
  every result that uses a `fail` outcome carries the assumption).
- **Assumption names a system may declare.** R-ASSUME's "unknown name" is read
  as anything but `info-in-place-v1`, `atomic-publish-v1`,
  `single-landing-v1`; `definite-fail-v1:<id>` is the adapter's, not the
  model's, so a system's list refuses it. A name listed twice is refused.
- **T-effect.** Section 9 lists no rejected fixture whose only fault is an
  untagged effect. PR 1 adds `rejected-untagged-effect.ops-v2` and the rule
  `T-effect` (4.7) for it.
- **The bridge for a v1 `rejected` request when the model commits.** There is
  no model label to bind; any `[fail [rejected L]]` then admits nothing, so
  the bridge binds the empty label.

Still open:

1. **Attempt identity.** Assigned as `Inv/step`, or written in the model (a
   stored attempt counter, a message id the real system sends)? The draft
   assigns it; an adapter then needs a mapping from the real identifier.
2. **Response values.** Not modeled, so "replay returns the original result"
   is weakened to "same outcome, no state change".
3. **At-most-once and in-flight rows.** H3-drained ends in `unsettled_effect`:
   the intended model never redelivers an in-flight row. Is reconciliation
   (query the receiver by intent) part of the intended model, and with what
   contract?
4. **Receiver contracts.** At-least-once with receiver dedupe by intent
   ("effectively once"): a third contract, or a clause on a receiver model?
5. **Late linearization bound.** Interleaving `info` commit points multiplies
   worlds. Its bound and reporting overlap R17.
6. **Intent namespace.** Duplicates count per `(channel, intent)`. Global, per
   channel, or per operation family?
7. **Classifier identity.** What a `<classifier-id>` names (code digest,
   configuration, version), and who reviews a classifier before its `fail`s
   are trusted.

## 12. Deferred (not R02)

These flip no probe test. They are recorded so the plan's rejected examples
have a home; they land with R04/R17, not R02.

- **Retry declarations**, `[retry C Max]`: the environment may re-invoke `C`
  with the same arguments after `info` or `fail [aborted]`, at most `Max`
  times. They constrain exploration only, never history well-formedness
  (4.4). Rejected example, *retry rule without attempt identity*: a retry of a
  v1 command. A command's intent is its invocation id, so each retry is a new
  intent, and no duplicate across retries can be stated; the rule would admit
  a retry only of an operation with `[intent X]`.
- **Frame claims**, `[disjoint A B]`: holds when `W(A) ∩ (R(B) ∪ W(B)) = ∅`,
  symmetrically, and no invariant reads both a table in `W(A)` and one in
  `W(B)`, with `R`/`W` from `caps.analyze` (`system.shen:233-277`) extended:
  `outbox T` writes `T`, `deliver N T` reads and writes `T`. Rejected example,
  *frame claiming disjointness while writing the outbox table*:
  `[disjoint "transfer" "relay-transfer"]` on the worked model, refuted by
  `transfer`'s `outbox` writing `transfer-outbox`, which `relay-transfer`
  reads and writes. A summary that treats `outbox` like v1 `publish` (empty
  write set) would accept it; that is the mutant this rule's PR must catch.
